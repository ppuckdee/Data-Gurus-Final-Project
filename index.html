    What to notice

    Cambodia rose from about 17% to 93%, the largest gain of any country (+76 points). Kenya and Bangladesh also gained more than 50 points. Chad was still below 10% in 2019 and gained less than 6 points in twenty years. Over the same period the world average went from about 79% to 90%.

    What have we done so far?

    We cleaned the World Bank electricity access data for 2000–2019 and removed regional summaries so we can compare individual countries. We built a line chart that shows how electricity access changed over time. Users can add or remove countries and hover over the chart to see exact values. We also published the website on GitHub Pages and added spaces for our planned maps and bar charts.

    What will be the most challenging part to design, and why?

    We expect the hardest part will be connecting the maps and charts so they work together. Choosing a country should update the related charts in a way that is easy to follow. Some countries have missing data, so we need to show those gaps clearly and keep our comparisons fair. We also need to arrange the charts and explanations so readers can understand where electricity access remains low and where it has improved.
